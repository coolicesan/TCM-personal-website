At a glanceSperm take over seventy days to develop and mature, and with transit time on top, any change in lifestyle generally takes about three months to show up in a semen analysis. The main factors affecting sperm quality are smoking, drinking, weight, prolonged exposure to heat, sleep and stress, and some medications — and many of them can be changed.

Discussion of trying to conceive has long centred on the woman, yet about a third of infertility is linked to the male partner. And the male tests are the simplest, the cheapest and the quickest to give results — one semen analysis can rule out a large share of the possibilities.

This article covers what the man can do: first what affects sperm, then what can genuinely be changed.

Tests for the Man: Do Them First, Don't Wait

There is only one core test: a semen analysis. No fasting, no blood draw, and results usually within one to two weeks.

If the first result is abnormal, don't jump to a conclusion — sperm take over seventy days to develop, and any fever, cold, stress or lack of sleep during that time can affect the result, so it's generally recommended to repeat the test a few weeks later.

For how to read the report and the common misunderstandings, see Why Can't I Get Pregnant? Common Causes and Tests.

Heat: The Easiest to Overlook and the Easiest to Change

The testicles sit outside the body precisely because sperm production needs an environment slightly cooler than body temperature. So keeping the scrotum hot for long periods affects sperm production.

Everyday sources of heat:

  • Long periods of sitting (drivers, office work, long-haul flights)
  • Putting a laptop directly on your lap
  • Frequent hot baths, saunas and hot tubs
  • Underwear or trousers that are too tight
  • Some work environments (kitchens, furnaces, outdoor work in high heat)

Practical advice: if you sit a lot, get up and walk around every hour; avoid putting a laptop on your lap; cut down on long soaks in hot water; choose looser, breathable underwear.

It should be said that the research here is mostly observational and the effect varies from person to person, so don't expect a big improvement from changing clothing alone. But these changes cost almost nothing and are worth making.

Anabolic Steroids: Worth Singling Out

Anabolic steroids used for bodybuilding suppress the body's own hormone production and can cause a large drop in sperm count, even complete absence of sperm, and recovery after stopping takes time and in some cases is incomplete.

This isn't rare among people in gym culture, but few bring it up at a fertility check. If you are using, or have used, them, be sure to tell your doctor — it directly affects how the results are read and the direction of management.

How to Improve It: A Practical List

  • Stop smoking — this is the factor with the clearest effect
  • Drink less alcohol
  • Control your weight — especially abdominal obesity
  • Improve your sleep — long-term lack of sleep affects the hormone axis
  • Exercise regularly but not excessively — too much endurance training may backfire
  • Reduce scrotal heat exposure
  • Stop anabolic steroids (under a doctor's guidance)
  • Review your current medications — some drugs may affect sperm; don't stop them yourself, ask your doctor first
  • Deal with long-term stress

The timetable: because the sperm production cycle is over seventy days, a change generally takes about three months before it may show up in a test. That also means that if you plan to improve things, the sooner you start the better — don't wait until you're about to do IVF.

Do Supplements Help?

There are many supplements on the market aimed at male fertility, mostly built around antioxidants. The current state of the research is that some trials suggest a possible positive effect on semen parameters, but study quality is generally low and results are inconsistent, and there isn't yet enough evidence to support routine use.

A more practical view:

  • If your diet is seriously unbalanced, topping up basic nutrients is reasonable
  • But supplements can't replace stopping smoking, controlling weight and improving sleep
  • Before taking any supplement, especially if you have a chronic illness or take medication, check with a healthcare professional

What Can Chinese Medicine Do?

It needs to be stated honestly: high-quality studies of male-factor infertility using pregnancy as the outcome are still limited, mostly small trials, from which no firm conclusion can be drawn.

A more reasonable way to position it:

  • Managing overall condition: fatigue, sleep, stress and digestion — these genuinely affect the hormone axis
  • Supporting lifestyle changes: the daily-living guidance of Chinese medicine points in the same direction as the evidence-based advice above
  • It can't replace necessary tests and treatment: varicocele, hormonal abnormalities, genetic factors and the like need to be handled by a urologist or andrologist

If the semen analysis is clearly abnormal, you should have a full assessment first, rather than spending three months on preparation and seeing what happens — especially when the female partner is older, as time itself is a factor.

When to See a Doctor

  • An abnormal semen analysis (still abnormal when repeated)
  • A past undescended testicle, testicular surgery, hernia surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy
  • Mumps with orchitis in the past
  • Testicular pain or swelling, or a lump you can feel — get seen promptly
  • Enlarged veins in the scrotum that feel like a "bag of worms" (possibly a varicocele)
  • Difficulty with erection or ejaculation
  • Current or past use of anabolic steroids
  • Not pregnant after a year of trying (six months if the female partner is over 35)

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I abstain before a semen analysis?

Generally 2 to 7 days. Too short and the sperm count comes out low; too long and motility may be affected. Follow the laboratory's specific instructions.

If one result isn't good, does that mean there's a problem?

Not necessarily. A single result is affected by fever, stress, sleep and the length of abstinence. It's generally recommended to repeat the test a few weeks later and let the doctor judge on the whole picture.

How long after changing my habits will I see a difference?

About three months. Sperm take over seventy days to develop and mature, so a change today won't show up in a test until about three months later. That's also why it's best not to start too late.

Does regular exercise affect sperm?

Regular, moderate exercise is generally beneficial. But extreme endurance training, together with the pressure and heat from long periods of cycling, may backfire. The key is intensity and recovery, not giving up exercise altogether.

Do oysters and zinc tablets help?

If your nutrient intake is low to begin with, topping up makes sense; but when your diet is balanced, the evidence for any extra benefit from supplements is limited. They can't offset the effects of smoking, obesity or lack of sleep.

Does the man need to see a Chinese medicine practitioner too?

If both partners want to work on their health, it makes sense to do so together — especially when the semen analysis is borderline, or there is marked fatigue, or problems with sleep and stress. But the same principle applies: get tested first, then work on your health.

Conclusion

The three most practical things for a man trying to conceive: have a semen analysis early, stop smoking and control your weight, and reduce scrotal heat exposure. All three cost very little, and they are among the few factors that are genuinely in your control.

Remember the time lag too — a change today won't be visible for about three months. So rather than waiting until the woman's tests are finished and you're about to start treatment, start together from the moment you decide to try for a baby.
