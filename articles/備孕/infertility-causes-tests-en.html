Why Can't I Get Pregnant?Common Causes, Which Tests to Have and How Chinese Medicine Can Help

If you haven't conceived after a year of trying (six months if you're 35 or over), you should be checked. A Hong Kong registered Chinese medicine practitioner explains the common causes of infertility in women and men, which tests to have, how to read a semen analysis report (WHO 2021 standards), and what Chinese medicine can and cannot do.

At a glanceIf you have had regular intercourse without contraception for a year and haven't conceived (six months if you are 35 or over), you should be checked. The causes are spread roughly evenly between the female partner, the male partner and both together, and a further group have no known cause — so both partners must be tested at the same time. Until the cause is clear, any treatment to build up your health is guesswork.

"We've been trying for a whole year with no news" — at this point the most common reaction is to start by looking after your health: drinking herbal soups, taking supplements, even seeing a Chinese medicine practitioner first. But that is actually the wrong order.

Blocked fallopian tubes won't open because you take herbs, and a serious sperm problem won't improve because the woman works on her health. Find out the cause first, then decide what to do, so that you don't waste time — and time is itself one of the most important variables here.

"Let's try a bit longer first" is the most common decision, and the most costly. If any of the above applies to you, the tests themselves aren't painful, but the time lost to delay can't be made up.

Which Partner Is the Cause?

The causes of infertility fall broadly into four groups, spread fairly evenly:

  • Female factors: problems with ovulation, the fallopian tubes, the uterus and so on
  • Male factors: sperm count, motility, shape or delivery problems
  • Both partners: a small problem on each side that together make conception difficult
  • Unexplained: all the standard tests are normal, yet pregnancy hasn't happened

A common mistake in practice is for only the woman to be tested. The male tests are the simplest, the cheapest and the quickest to give results — a single semen analysis rules out a large share of the possibilities, so there is no reason not to do it first.

Ovulation problemsPMOS (formerly PCOS), hypothalamic amenorrhoea (excessive exercise, low weight, stress), thyroid disorders, hyperprolactinaemia, premature ovarian insufficiency

Two of these deserve particular attention:

  • Ovulation problems often leave clues. Persistently irregular cycles, cycles longer than 35 days, or fewer than 8 periods a year usually mean there is a problem with ovulation (see PMOS (Formerly PCOS)).
  • Tubal problems often have no symptoms at all. Many women only learn they once had an infection when they are tested.

Common Causes in Men

CategoryCommon conditions
Problems making spermVaricocele, a history of undescended testicles, genetic or chromosomal factors, hormone imbalance, mumps with orchitis
Blockage in transportBlocked or absent vas deferens, a previous vasectomy, blockage after infection
Ejaculation problemsRetrograde ejaculation, difficulty with erection or ejaculation
Lifestyle and environmentSmoking, heavy drinking, obesity, prolonged exposure to heat (long periods of sitting, saunas, laptops on the lap), certain medications, anabolic steroids

Anabolic steroids (used for bodybuilding) deserve a mention of their own: they suppress the body's own hormone production and can cause a large drop in sperm count, even complete absence of sperm, and recovery after stopping takes time. This isn't rare among people in gym culture, yet it is often overlooked.

Unexplained Infertility

When both partners' standard tests are all normal and pregnancy still hasn't happened, it is classed as "unexplained infertility".

The label is discouraging, but what it actually means is: no problem has been found with the tests available, not that there is no problem. It may involve egg quality, the fertilisation process or embryo implantation, none of which can currently be tested routinely.

The good news is that the direction of management is fairly clear for this group — international guidelines generally suggest starting with ovulation stimulation combined with intrauterine insemination (IUI), and only then considering IVF (see IVF Step by Step).

An important reminder about AMH: AMH reflects the number of eggs, not their quality, and it cannot predict the chance of natural conception. A low value doesn't mean you can't get pregnant (see Does a Low AMH Mean You Can't Get Pregnant?).

Which Tests Should the Man Have?

There is only one core test: a semen analysis. It's simple, cheap, quick, and carries a great deal of information.

Preparing for the test

  • Abstain from ejaculation for 2 to 7 days (too short or too long both affect the result)
  • Avoid testing straight after a fever, a cold or a period of severe stress
  • Collect and transport the sample as the laboratory instructs

WHO 2021 reference values

ParameterLower reference limit
Semen volume1.4 mL
Sperm concentration16 million per mL
Total sperm number per ejaculate39 million
Total motility42%
Progressive motility30%
Vitality (live sperm)54%
Normal forms4%

Three points you need to understand

1. These are statistical reference lines, not pass marks. The values come from the 5th percentile of about 3,500 men whose partners conceived naturally within a year. In other words, 5% of men who can father a child naturally fall below this line. Being below the reference value doesn't mean you can't father a child, and being above it doesn't guarantee there is no problem.

2. One result isn't enough to judge by. Sperm take over seventy days to develop and mature, and any fever, stress or lack of sleep during that time can affect the result. If the first result is abnormal, it is generally recommended to repeat the test a few weeks later before drawing a conclusion.

3. Morphology is the most easily misunderstood item. "Only 4% normal forms" sounds alarming, but that is exactly the lower reference limit for men who can father a child naturally — it is normal for the great majority of sperm to be imperfect in shape. Shape alone isn't enough to judge fertility.

If the semen analysis is clearly abnormal, the next steps may include hormone blood tests, a scrotal ultrasound (to check for a varicocele) or, in severe cases, genetic testing, and a referral to a urologist or andrologist.

Reading Your Results: Three Common Misconceptions

Misconception 1: "Everything's normal, so there's nothing wrong." Normal standard tests can still mean unexplained infertility. Normal means the common problems have been ruled out; it doesn't mean you can wait indefinitely — especially as age is still ticking on.

Misconception 2: "One bad number means there's no hope." Whether it's AMH or a semen analysis, no single value decides the outcome. Look at the whole picture, and read it together with age and medical history.

Misconception 3: "Once the tests are done, I'll get my body in shape first." The direction is right, but don't forget the time. If your doctor has already advised starting treatment, don't postpone it for the sake of "three months of preparation first" — and this matters even more at an older age.

What Can Chinese Medicine Do, and What Can't It Do?

This section needs to be clear, because wrongly set expectations are the easiest way to waste time.

What it can't do

  • It can't open blocked fallopian tubes. Structural problems need surgery, or to be bypassed directly with IVF.
  • It can't repair a serious problem with sperm production. For example genetic causes or azoospermia.
  • It can't increase the number of follicles in the ovaries. The number of follicles is set at birth and declines with age, and that is irreversible.
  • It can't replace tests. This is the most important point — starting treatment without finding out the cause is working with a blindfold on.

Where there is room to help

  • Regulating ovulation and the menstrual cycle: irregular cycles and erratic ovulation are the area Chinese medicine more commonly works on.
  • Mood, sleep and stress: these genuinely affect the hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis, and are the part patients feel most directly.
  • Working alongside assisted reproduction: meta-analyses of Chinese-language studies suggest that Chinese herbal medicine alongside IVF may reduce the incidence of ovarian hyperstimulation and improve the clinical pregnancy rate, though study quality has room to improve (see How Does Chinese Medicine Work Alongside an IVF Cycle?).
  • Adjusting the man's lifestyle factors: sleep, weight, hot environments, smoking and drinking — these have a real effect on sperm quality, and can be changed.

The state of the evidence, honestly stated

High-quality studies using fertility outcomes as the measure are still limited. A Cochrane review of Chinese herbal medicine for subfertile women with PMOS (then called polycystic ovary syndrome) found that the existing evidence isn't sufficient to support that use, and none of the included studies reported the live birth rate. Results from Chinese-language studies on working alongside assisted reproduction are more positive, but study quality is low, as the authors themselves note.

So the reasonable way to position it is: Chinese medicine is part of overall management, not a shortcut around tests and treatment.

When to See a Doctor Without Delay

  • You already meet the timing criteria above
  • No period for three months or more
  • Cycles persistently longer than 35 days, or fewer than 8 periods a year
  • Severe period pain or pain during sex (endometriosis needs to be ruled out)
  • A past history of pelvic inflammatory disease, a sexually transmitted infection, or pelvic or abdominal surgery
  • Two or more miscarriages
  • The male partner has had testicular surgery, an undescended testicle, chemotherapy, or marked testicular swelling or pain
  • The male partner is using, or has used, anabolic steroids

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should we try before getting tested?

Generally one year if you're under 35, six months if you're 35 or over, and seeing a doctor straight away if you're 40 or over. If you already know of a menstrual problem, a history of pelvic surgery or a relevant history in the male partner, don't wait.

Do both of us really have to be tested?

Yes. The causes are spread roughly evenly between the two partners, so testing only one means seeing only half the picture. The semen analysis is the simplest, cheapest and quickest of all the tests, so there is no reason to delay it.

If a semen analysis is below the standard, does that mean he can't father a child?

No. The WHO reference values are the 5th percentile of men who can father a child naturally — a statistical benchmark, not a pass mark — and there are cases of natural conception below that line. A single result is also affected by fever, stress and sleep, so it generally needs repeating before a judgement is made.

Does "unexplained infertility" mean it can't be treated?

No. It means no clear cause has been found with the tests available, not that there is no direction for management. International guidelines generally suggest starting with ovulation stimulation plus intrauterine insemination, and only then considering IVF.

Should I see a Chinese medicine practitioner first, or get tested first?

Get tested first. Once the cause is clear, you know whether Chinese medicine care is appropriate and what it should address. If you already know there is a tubal blockage or a serious male factor, working on your health first will only waste time.

How long does it take for Chinese medicine care to show whether it's working?

Generally you think in months, not weeks, and usually need to observe two to three menstrual cycles. But that timetable has to be considered alongside the overall treatment plan — if you're older or your doctor has advised starting treatment, don't postpone it for the sake of preparation.

What can the man do to improve sperm quality?

Stop smoking, cut down on alcohol, control his weight, improve his sleep, avoid prolonged exposure to heat (long periods of sitting, saunas, a laptop on the lap) and avoid anabolic steroids. Sperm take over seventy days to develop, so after making changes it takes about three months before they may show up in a test.

Conclusion

When you haven't conceived for a long time, the most effective first step is not a tonic but finding out the cause, with both partners tested together. The tests themselves aren't complicated, but they decide whether every decision afterwards is well spent.

Chinese medicine has a place in regulating the cycle, mood and sleep, lifestyle factors and working alongside assisted reproduction, but it can't replace tests and it can't deal with structural problems. Putting it inside the overall plan, not ahead of it, is the sensible way to use it.
